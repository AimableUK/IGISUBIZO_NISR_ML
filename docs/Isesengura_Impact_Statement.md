# How Mboni Helps People in Rwanda

## Overview

Mboni is a data-informed decision-support and analytical workspace designed to help people understand Rwanda's development challenges and make better-informed decisions. It turns relevant public statistics into accessible explanations, comparisons, charts, tables, maps, and transparent analytical views.

The goal is not to make decisions on people's behalf. Mboni helps users explore what the available evidence says, compare options, understand trade-offs, and identify questions that deserve further investigation.

The project aligns with the purpose of the NISR Big Data Hackathon: using NISR data and other suitable public datasets to develop practical, data-driven solutions connected to Rwanda's development priorities. Potential applications span employment, education, agriculture, poverty reduction, financial inclusion, health, business activity, and other areas where reliable evidence can support decisions.

**Important:** The exact coverage and reliability of any result depend on the datasets actually obtained, checked, and integrated. A dataset category mentioned here is a potential analytical input, not a claim that the data is already integrated or available at every geographic or demographic level.

## 1. Who Mboni Is Designed to Help

Mboni can support different people and institutions, each with their own questions and decisions:

- **Students and school leavers** exploring education, training, and career pathways.
- **Job seekers and career changers** trying to understand employment conditions, sector trends, and skill requirements.
- **Farmers and agricultural cooperatives** comparing production patterns, agricultural conditions, and potential risks.
- **Small-business owners and entrepreneurs** exploring local economic activity and conditions relevant to business planning.
- **Households and community organizations** seeking to understand poverty, access to services, and socioeconomic conditions.
- **Researchers, educators, and journalists** exploring public data and communicating findings clearly.
- **Civil-society organizations and development partners** identifying needs, comparing local conditions, and examining potential intervention areas.
- **Public officials and policy teams** exploring evidence relevant to planning, service delivery, and resource allocation.

Mboni makes public evidence easier to explore. It does not replace professional advice, official statistics, field research, or the responsibilities of decision-makers.

## 2. Employment, Careers, and Skills

Mboni can help users investigate Rwanda's labour market beyond headline unemployment figures. Employment outcomes, labour-force participation, underemployment, job quality, skills, and access to opportunities are related but distinct issues.

Depending on the availability and quality of integrated sources, users may be able to explore:

- Employment, unemployment, labour-force participation, and labour underutilization.
- Differences by age, sex, residence, district, education, occupation, and economic sector where the source supports those breakdowns.
- Changes in employment indicators over time.
- The distribution of work across agriculture, industry, services, and other reported sectors.
- Available evidence about education, vocational training, qualifications, and transitions into work.
- Differences between labour-market supply indicators and business or sector activity.
- Youth employment and young people not in education, employment, or training, when suitable data is available.
- Wage and job-quality indicators only where reliable source data provides them.

These views can help students and job seekers ask more informed questions about career paths, and help educators, training providers, employers, and development organizations examine patterns that may deserve attention.

**Mboni should not claim without evidence** that a specific career guarantees employment, that advertised vacancies represent the entire labour market, or that two datasets can be directly combined when their definitions, time periods, or geographic levels do not match.

## 3. Agriculture and Food Security

Agriculture is important to livelihoods, household welfare, food availability, and Rwanda's wider economy. Mboni can help users explore agricultural statistics and the conditions associated with different outcomes.

Where suitable official or otherwise credible data is available, analysis may cover:

- Crop production and yield trends.
- Differences between crops, seasons, and geographic areas.
- Reported agricultural practices and input use, including irrigation, fertilizer, improved seeds, and crop protection where recorded.
- Agricultural land use and production patterns.
- Food-security and vulnerability indicators.
- Relationships between agricultural outcomes and relevant household or socioeconomic measures.
- Changes over time that may identify questions for agricultural extension, research, or planning.

Farmers, cooperatives, researchers, and development partners could use these analyses to compare reported conditions and investigate potential opportunities or risks.

Historical patterns alone cannot determine which crop a particular farmer should plant. Crop decisions also depend on current weather, soil, water, prices, input costs, market access, local expertise, and the farmer's circumstances. Mboni should label forecasts as estimates, not guarantees.

## 4. Poverty, Household Welfare, and Financial Inclusion

Income, poverty, access to financial services, and household living conditions influence people's ability to participate in economic activity and withstand shocks. Mboni can help make these issues easier to examine together when compatible datasets are available.

Potential areas of analysis include:

- Poverty and household welfare indicators.
- Differences in living conditions across geographic areas and population groups.
- Access to financial services and reported financial behaviours.
- Access to basic services such as electricity, water, sanitation, and digital connectivity, where measured.
- Food insecurity and vulnerability indicators.
- Patterns related to livelihoods, household resources, and economic participation.
- Comparisons over time, provided the measures are sufficiently consistent.

These analyses may help community organizations, researchers, development partners, and planning teams understand where conditions differ and which questions need further investigation.

Mboni should not infer an individual's financial status, eligibility for support, or personal circumstances from aggregate statistics. It should not claim that a program caused an outcome unless the available study design supports that conclusion.

## 5. Education and Human Capital

Education data can help people understand participation, learning outcomes, qualifications, and the relationship between education and later opportunities.

Where source data supports it, Mboni may help users explore:

- Education participation and completion indicators.
- Examination outcomes by subject, level, year, or geographic area.
- Differences between education pathways, including general education and technical or vocational education.
- Available indicators on training outcomes and transitions into employment.
- Differences in outcomes across population groups or locations.
- How education indicators change over time.

Students and families can use these findings as context for further research. Education providers and planners can use them to identify patterns that warrant investigation.

Examination performance does not by itself determine an individual's ability or future career success. A relationship between a training pathway and employment outcomes does not prove that the pathway alone caused those outcomes.

## 6. Health and Demographic Wellbeing

Public health and demographic statistics can reveal broad patterns in population needs and wellbeing. When relevant, sufficiently documented datasets are integrated, Mboni can support exploration of:

- Population size, structure, and demographic change.
- Maternal, child, and general health indicators available in public sources.
- Access to health services and differences between areas, where measured.
- Relevant household conditions and their relationship to health indicators.
- Differences across time and population groups supported by the source data.

These analyses are intended for understanding population-level patterns and supporting research or planning. They are not a diagnostic service, do not provide individual medical advice, and should not identify individuals from anonymized or aggregate data.

## 7. Business Activity and Local Economic Conditions

Entrepreneurs and existing businesses often need to understand the economic environment before deciding what to investigate or where to invest. Mboni can help users explore available evidence about business activity and the wider economy.

Potential analytical views include:

- Establishment counts and the distribution of businesses by sector and location, where available.
- Changes in establishment counts across comparable census rounds.
- Economic activity and employment by sector.
- Regional differences in reported economic indicators.
- Relevant population, infrastructure, poverty, or household indicators that provide context for local planning.
- Comparisons between areas using clearly defined measures.

This can help entrepreneurs form better research questions and help researchers or development organizations examine patterns in local economic activity.

Business counts or population size alone cannot establish that a location is commercially viable. A real business decision also needs current information about demand, competition, rents, supply chains, regulation, costs, and customer behaviour.

## 8. Local and Regional Comparisons

Mboni aims to make geographic differences easier to explore, including district-level comparisons when source data supports that resolution.

Users may be able to:

- Compare reported indicators across districts or other available geographic units.
- Explore differences between urban and rural areas where the source provides those categories.
- View indicators by province or national level where applicable.
- Examine how a measure changes over time.
- Compare several indicators side by side instead of relying on a single headline number.
- Identify data gaps and differences in reporting coverage.

Rwanda has 30 districts across five provinces, including Kigali City. However, **Mboni must not imply that every dataset covers all 30 districts**. Geographic coverage varies by source, survey design, year, and indicator. Maps and comparisons should clearly show missing areas, suppressed values, and differences in coverage.

## 9. Evidence-Based Planning and Development

Researchers, public institutions, civil-society organizations, and development partners may use Mboni to explore questions relevant to planning and service delivery.

Depending on the available evidence, the workspace can help users:

- Identify geographic areas with comparatively high or low reported values for a defined indicator.
- Compare needs and outcomes across population groups.
- Track changes in selected indicators over time.
- Explore whether multiple indicators move together.
- Review assumptions, source definitions, and limitations before drawing conclusions.
- Identify where additional data collection or field research may be needed.
- Communicate findings through charts, tables, maps, and concise explanations.

Mboni supports analysis; it does not automatically determine where public funding should go or rank communities by their worthiness. Any prioritization should use transparent, agreed criteria and appropriate expert review.

## 10. An Analytical Workspace, Not Just a Chatbot

Mboni is designed around the exploration of evidence, not just conversational answers.

A user should be able to ask a question and receive a structured analytical view, such as:

- Key indicators and summary metrics.
- Charts showing trends, distributions, or comparisons.
- Tables that preserve exact values.
- Maps where geographic data is suitable.
- Explanations of the main observed patterns.
- Source information, definitions, and data-quality notes.
- Clear distinctions between observed values, calculations, estimates, and projections.

Users should be able to explore and customize supported visualizations, revisit previous analyses, and understand which data and assumptions produced a result. Changing the appearance of a chart should not silently change the underlying analytical result.

The system should show source provenance and uncertainty so users can inspect the evidence rather than being asked to trust an unexplained AI-generated answer.

## 11. Responsible Analysis and Trust

Trustworthiness is central to Mboni's purpose. Its results should follow these principles:

- **Source-grounded results:** Do not invent datasets, statistics, citations, model outputs, or confidence levels.
- **Traceable evidence:** Show the source, reporting period, geographic coverage, units, and definitions where known.
- **Clear uncertainty:** Identify missing data, small samples, stale information, incompatible definitions, and other material limitations.
- **Separation of evidence and interpretation:** Distinguish source-reported values from calculations, interpretations, and forecasts.
- **No unsupported causation:** Correlation or co-movement does not establish that one factor caused another.
- **Transparent methods:** Explain calculations and analytical methods in language appropriate to the user.
- **Human agency:** Present evidence and trade-offs without claiming that one option is guaranteed to succeed.
- **Privacy protection:** Avoid exposing personal information or enabling identification of people from sensitive or granular data.
- **Accessible presentation:** Make findings understandable through readable text, accessible charts, data tables, keyboard support, and text alternatives for audio content.

If a source cannot support a question, Mboni should explain the limitation and suggest a narrower question that can be answered responsibly.

## 12. Data Coverage and Verification Status

Mboni may draw on NISR datasets and other credible public sources, including relevant government data portals and domain-specific publications. Potential sources include labour-force surveys, establishment statistics, household and poverty surveys, agricultural surveys, financial-inclusion research, education statistics, health and demographic surveys, population data, and food-security assessments.

However, a source being publicly listed does not necessarily mean its underlying records are downloadable, openly licensed, current, complete, or suitable for integration. Each source must be checked for:

- Access and reuse conditions.
- Data format and availability.
- Reporting period and update frequency.
- Geographic and demographic coverage.
- Definitions, units, and sampling methodology.
- Missing values and data quality.
- Compatibility with other datasets.
- Whether values are official observations, derived indicators, or estimates.

**Implementation status must be stated honestly.** If a demo uses synthetic, generated, proxy, or illustrative data, its charts and findings must be labelled accordingly. Such results must not be presented as verified official statistics. A dataset should only be described as integrated after the actual source has been obtained, validated, documented, and connected to the analytical pipeline.

## 13. How Mboni Fits the NISR Big Data Hackathon

The NISR Big Data Hackathon encourages data-driven solutions aligned with Rwanda's development priorities and requires the use of NISR data. Mboni's approach is to make public statistics more useful for practical questions through analysis, visualization, comparison, and transparent interpretation.

Its potential relevance spans the hackathon's three tracks:

- **Agricultural Productivity:** Explore agricultural production, yield, inputs, food security, and relevant geographic patterns where reliable data is available.
- **Financial Inclusion and Poverty Reduction:** Examine financial access, household welfare, poverty, vulnerability, and differences between areas or groups.
- **Open Innovation:** Investigate other national-development questions, potentially including employment, education, health, population, business activity, and access to services.

The final hackathon submission should identify the exact problem being addressed, the NISR dataset or datasets actually used, the analytical method, the intended beneficiaries, and the evidence supporting any claimed impact. A broad platform is not a substitute for a clearly demonstrated, useful solution to a defined problem.

## 14. Intended Impact

Mboni aims to reduce the distance between published statistics and practical understanding. Its intended contributions are to:

1. Make complex public data easier for non-specialists to explore.
2. Help people compare options using evidence rather than generic advice alone.
3. Make regional and population-level differences more visible.
4. Support students, job seekers, farmers, entrepreneurs, researchers, and development organizations in asking better questions.
5. Help institutions identify patterns that may deserve further investigation.
6. Improve transparency by making sources, assumptions, and limitations visible.
7. Encourage responsible use of data in decisions that affect communities.

These are intended benefits, not claims of measured impact. Any statement that Mboni has improved employment, increased farm yields, reduced poverty, or changed policy outcomes must be supported by an appropriate evaluation rather than inferred from product features or demo results.

## Summary

Mboni helps people explore Rwanda's public data to better understand employment, skills, agriculture, food security, poverty, financial inclusion, education, health, demographics, and local economic conditions. It turns available evidence into interactive analytical views that users can inspect, compare, and revisit.

Its core promise is straightforward: **make data easier to understand, make analysis easier to explore, and make decisions better informed by evidence.**
